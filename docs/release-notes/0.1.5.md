This is **0.1.5**, after [0.1.4](https://github.com/bnelair/mef3io-studio/releases/tag/v0.1.4): Join or trim checks much faster on big recordings and network drives, you always choose the channels (the ones you looked at in the viewer come ticked), and it uses mef3io 2.3.0, which fixes a case where a join with a time window could miss data (see below).

## Please redo some joins made with 0.1.4

**If you made a recording with Join or trim in Studio 0.1.4 and chose a time
window (keep only a part) for a recording whose saved segment times were
wrong, the new recording may be missing data.** Some recordings carry wrong
start or end times in their file headers (for example after a clock problem);
0.1.4 could then drop that recording's chosen part without saying so. The
library behind Studio (mef3io 2.3.0) fixes this, and 0.1.5 uses it. Please
make those recordings again with 0.1.5 and compare their length with what you
expect. Joins without a time window, and recordings with correct times, were
not affected. The originals were never changed.

## Fixed

- **Studio now uses mef3io 2.3.0** (it was 2.2.0): the fix above, and fewer file requests when a short part of many
  recordings is copied, which matters most on network drives.

- **"Checking everything before starting" in Join or trim does less work
  on big recordings.** Studio no longer opens every recording again or walks
  through all of its folders (that took tens of thousands of file requests).
  It uses what Explore already knows about each recording (when it starts and
  ends, its channels and its size), and touches each recording at most once
  per action: it looks at a few files only to see whether the recording has
  changed since Explore read it, which includes a recording that has grown
  while the page was open. If it has changed, the page says so, checks again
  and uses the new times. The library's own check and copy still read the
  recording (once in the check, once in the run); how much work they do is
  improved in the library's own release, not here. No time saving is promised.
- **Sending a range from the signal viewer to Join or trim now keeps your
  channel choice.** Join or trim stops at the **Channels** step, with the
  channels you could see in the viewer kept and the ones you hid left out, so
  you can see and change which channels go into the new recording. If you
  hid every channel, none is ticked yet and the step says so.

## Changed

- **Join or trim tells you about channels with different units or amplitude
  settings.** After saving, the result says "Some channels have different units
  in different recordings" (the whole channel is labelled with the units of its
  first part, and nothing was rescaled) and "Some channels were recorded with
  different amplitude settings in different recordings" (the values are right
  in Studio, but some older programs, for example the mef_tools Python package,
  show those channels as all zeros), naming the channels. Mixed units no longer stop
  the join.

- **Choosing a few channels is easier to find.** The channel step now says
  that a ticked box means "kept" and explains how to keep only a few: choose
  **Exclude all**, then tick the channels you want.
