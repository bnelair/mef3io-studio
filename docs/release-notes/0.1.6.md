This is **0.1.6**, after [0.1.5](https://github.com/bnelair/mef3io-studio/releases/tag/v0.1.5): the signal viewer is made for recordings of several days, **nothing you change in a recording's notes is written until you press "Write to file"**, stored notes can be removed or reworded safely, every wizard has one button to start, and Studio uses mef3io 2.4.0.

Installers for Windows, macOS (14 or later) and Linux are attached below,
with the user manual (PDF) and the tutorial videos. Check your download
against the `SHA256SUMS` file.

> **These builds are not code-signed yet.** Your computer will ask you to
> confirm the first start:
>
> - **Windows:** "Windows protected your PC" → *More info* → *Run anyway*.
> - **Mac:** open the app once and close the warning, then go to *System
>   Settings → Privacy & Security* and click *Open Anyway* next to the message
>   about mef3io-studio. (On macOS 14: right-click the app → *Open*.) If
>   macOS says the app "is damaged and can't be opened", the download was
>   quarantined: move the app to *Applications*, run
>   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/mef3io-studio.app` in
>   Terminal, then open it again.
> - **Linux:** make the AppImage executable (*Properties → Allow executing*),
>   then double-click it.

## What is new

### Notes: nothing is written until "Write to file"

Every change to the notes in the viewer (a new note, a reworded one, a stored
note taken out) stays in the viewer window until you press **Write to file**.

- A **red bar at the top of the viewer** says the changes are NOT SAVED and how
  many there are ("1 added, 1 changed, 1 removed"). It is the one place to write
  or discard, and it stays when you leave edit mode.
- **Write to file** (or Ctrl+S) first looks at the recording again and then shows
  a list of every change: what is added, what is changed (old and new wording),
  what is removed, in which recording, and when. Only after you choose **Write N
  changes to the file** is anything touched; **Keep editing** leaves everything as
  it is. If another program changed a note you marked, the list says so and
  nothing can be written until you open the recording again.
- **Removing a stored note only marks it** (struck through). Delete again, the
  menu, or Ctrl+Z brings it back. A note stored twice asks whether to mark one
  copy or all.
- **Marking many notes is easy and fast:** in the notes list, Shift or Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) click
  and Ctrl+A (⌘A) select several notes, Delete marks them all in one step, **Keep**
  brings them back. It stays quick with 50,000 notes.
- After a write the viewer says exactly what is in the file ("Written to the
  recording: 1 added, 1 changed, 1 removed"). If a write stops half way, it says
  what was written (that cannot be undone) and what is still only in the window.
- Closing the window, quitting the app, or starting a task that copies, joins,
  packs or writes recordings (the batch runs, Join or trim, pack, saving a new
  recording with changes) asks first: **Write to file**, **Discard changes** or
  **Stay**. Leaving edit mode no longer asks or discards anything.

Only the notes of a recording change when you write them: the signal and the
details are never touched, and a removed note cannot be brought back from the
app, so keep a copy of an original you may need.

### The signal viewer, made for long recordings

The viewer now copes with recordings of several days, many channels and
network drives. What you will notice:

- **Overview of the whole recording.** A band above the coloured timeline bar
  shows the lowest and highest value of a channel over the whole recording, so
  you can see where to go before the slow loading of a page. The frame of the
  current page reaches up over the band, and clicking or dragging on it moves
  there. The **Overview** button chooses the channels (the first shown by
  default, up to four, each scaled to its own size); channels you hide leave
  the band. It is read in the background from the recording that is already
  open, never loading the signals. The band and the timeline bar are read
  together, once, after the first page is up and its neighbours are loaded;
  paging never reads them again.
- **Opens at once.** Opening a recording no longer inspects it first; the
  first page appears as soon as it is read, and the pages next to it (four
  ahead, one behind) are prepared while you look. When you are idle for a
  moment, it prepares a few more in the direction you were going.
- **Quicker paging.** Turning a page, changing the montage or a filter reads
  nothing it already has. While a page loads, the picture you had stays,
  paler and with arrows, so it never looks like the new page; a small spinner
  shows in the corner after a short wait.
- **Readable when it opens.** Every row is centred on its own level and the
  sensitivity is chosen for you; where a trace is cut off at the edge of its
  band, it is marked, and what was taken off is said. Rows that are not EEG
  (ECG, EMG, EOG, DC, SpO2, pulse, triggers) have a sensitivity of their own.
  Your own choice is never overridden.
- **Many channels.** The viewer shows "Rows 33-64 of 256" with a scroll bar and
  **Fit all rows**, and groups channels into leads (a lead is a name with its
  contacts); channels that are not EEG come last.
- **Time on screen** is shown beside the timebase ("= 10 s", "1 day 6 h"), the
  timebase goes down to days, and you can choose **colours of your own** for the
  traces and the background.
- **Moving through days.** The timeline bar shows where there is data (a pause
  inside a recording is a gap), the dates at midnight, and what is under the
  pointer ("There is data here" or "No data: a gap of 3 h"). Ctrl+Right /
  Ctrl+Left move one hour and Ctrl+Shift+Right / Left one day (on a Mac:
  Alt+Shift+Right / Left and Command+Option+Right / Left), and `.` / `,` jump to the next or
  previous stretch with data. **Go** takes a date and time. (The faint picture
  of one channel that the bar used to draw is gone: the overview band above it
  shows the same, better.)

### Notes in the viewer

- **Add a note fast.** `N` opens a small text field at the pointer (Enter keeps
  the note, Esc drops it); Ctrl+S opens the list to write it. It keeps the page you
  are on and works for recordings with tens of thousands of notes.
- **No note is stored twice.** A note equal to a stored or waiting one (same
  time, text and kind) is refused when you make it, and a write never stores one
  twice.
- **A note that is already stored can be reworded.** The new wording appears next
  to the old one (struck through) and replaces it when you write; only plain notes
  and events with a length can be reworded (other kinds can be removed, not
  changed), and the note stays in its own recording. If a recording cannot be
  changed (it needs both passwords, it is a single-file recording, its folder is
  read-only, another program is changing it, an earlier change was left
  unfinished, its notes were changed since you opened it), a message says why and
  what to do, and exactly what was and was not written.
- A recording whose notes were being changed when a program stopped opens with its
  signal, and the notes list says why the notes are not shown and what to do.
- The list and the search stay quick with 60,000 notes; a crowded page merges
  notes that fall in the same place on screen.

### Network drives and Windows

- **The viewer keeps the files it is reading open between pages** (up to a number
  chosen from your channels and the limits of your computer: at most 128 on Linux and
  Windows, 32 on macOS; the Windows and macOS numbers are assumed, not measured), so
  paging no longer opens the same files again and again; this matters most on a network
  drive. It lets go of them as soon as you close the window, before it writes notes,
  before anything else the app starts (renaming, joining, anonymizing, packing), and
  after 8 seconds without reading. While you are reading, a recording is held open by
  design. **On Windows, a recording shown in a viewer cannot be moved or renamed by
  another program while it is being read** (Windows does not allow it while a file in
  the folder is open); it is let go of 8 seconds after the last read.
- The viewer's controls above the signal now wrap onto as many rows as the window width
  needs (two on a wide screen), instead of two toolbars that lost their last buttons to
  a ">>" menu in a narrow window or with a large font. Nothing is hidden by width any more.

### Wizards and Check for problems

- **One button to start, in every wizard.** Anonymize, Join or trim, Make new,
  Cut into pieces and Edit details no longer have a separate "check" button
  to press, wait for, and then press again. The one button (for example
  **Save the new recording**) first checks everything without writing
  anything ("Step 1 of 2", on the page that follows, with the time since it
  started and **Stop**), and then makes the result ("Step 2 of 2"). What is
  written is exactly what was checked. If something stops it, every reason
  is listed in plain words and you are back at the last step with every
  choice kept; **Stop** in the check does the same, and a Stop you pressed
  always wins, even when the check was just finishing.
- **After a run.** **Back to my choices** keeps everything you chose, also
  after Stop, and **Show the last result** brings the outcome back until the
  next run really starts writing. A refusal or a Stop in step 1 never takes
  the last result (or **Open it in Explore**) away. Anonymize asks first when
  the key to the moved dates was not saved.
- **Passwords are never skipped by accident.** They are cleared when a run
  starts and when you leave the page. After that the step does not go on
  until you type them again or click a choice, "No password" included, and
  says why. Make new and Cut into pieces cannot be closed with X or Close
  while they work, and nothing is wiped by trying; **Stop** comes first.
- **Check for problems says what it really checks.** The tab offers **Quick
  look**, **Full check** and **Read everything back**, shows about how much
  each reads, a bar and **Stop**. The first two do not read the signal
  values themselves, and every "No problems" of theirs says so; only **Read
  everything back** can find damage inside the signal (it reads about as
  much as copying the recording). Results name the kind of check they come
  from.

### Library

- **Studio now uses mef3io 2.4.0** (it was 2.3.0). What you notice: when you
  open a recording whose notes and events were being changed when a program
  was interrupted, **Explore** says the notes and events cannot be read right
  now (instead of showing none), **Check for problems** says what happened
  and what to do next (the recording must be rescued before its notes and
  events can be used again; this app version does not do that itself yet),
  and Join or trim, Edit details and Anonymize refuse it in words that fit
  (nothing is written). Leftover files from such a change are reported too.
  Nothing else changes yet.

## What to try

- Open a recording in the viewer, turn on edit mode (`N`), add a note, remove a
  stored note (Delete), and press **Write to file**: check that the list shows
  what you did and that the file holds exactly that afterwards. Try **Keep
  editing** and **Discard changes…** too.
- Select many notes in the list (Ctrl+A) and mark them in one step, then bring
  some back with **Keep**.
- Page through a long recording and use the **Overview** band to find where to go.
- Start a wizard with one button and press **Stop** in step 1.

## Known limitations

- **Not tried on a real network drive.** The viewer's changes for network drives
  (keeping files open between pages, fewer file requests) are built and tested
  on a local disk only. Tell us how it behaves on yours.
- **On Windows, a recording shown in a viewer cannot be moved or renamed by
  another program while it is being read** (see above; it is let go of 8 seconds
  after the last read). The open-file limits for Windows and macOS are assumed,
  not measured.
- **Writing notes was tested with made-up recordings on Linux.** Windows and macOS
  were not tried by hand.
- **A recording whose notes were left half changed by an interrupted program
  cannot be repaired in Studio yet**; Studio says what happened and what to do.
- **A single-file recording (`.mefd.tar`) cannot get changes to its notes**:
  unpack it first.
- The builds are not code-signed yet (see above).

## How to report a problem without sharing patient data

Open an issue at
[github.com/bnelair/mef3io-studio/issues](https://github.com/bnelair/mef3io-studio/issues)
and say what you did, what you expected and what happened, with the version
(Help → About). **Do not attach recordings, screenshots of patient details, note
text or file names that hold a name.** The app writes no patient data to its logs;
**Help → Open the log folder** shows them, and you can attach a log after looking
through it. If a message offers "Technical details", those are safe to copy.
